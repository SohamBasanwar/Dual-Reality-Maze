Removable carriers and keepers

The Architect button reaches the breadboard

The previous lid-mounted switch cassette is replaced with a long printed stem and a removable two-bearing guide. Pressing the outer cap presses the actual switch down; its spring returns the cap. A flange stop limits travel. Main retains its short power-control cassette.

Nominal dimensions: breadboard top Z15, switch actuator Z22.3, stem tip Z22.45 mm. Free gap is 0.15 mm and drawn travel is 0.45 mm. These settings must be adjusted to the actual installed switch height and stroke. Do not let an overlong stem hold the switch on at rest. Use fit samples 15–17 before printing a full lid.

The photoresistor dome opens

The opaque floor separates the sensor from internal lighting. Only two small lead passages enter the case; seal around the real leads if light leaks through. The thin printed roof remains an optical test. Use the separate clear-film alternative if your filament blocks ambient light.

The castle gate returns

The arched portcullis and visible lifting motion are back. Its drive now sits inside the controller. The guard reaches 40 mm above the 58 mm lid plane, compared with 60 mm for the earlier guard: a 20 mm reduction. The gate travels about 12.12 mm through its nominal range.

The closed and open views use actual CAD parts and their calculated travel. Horn fit, tiny pin durability, printed latch strength, pinch access and loaded servo operation still need physical tests. The flat rotary-window concept remains available in Revision 07 for comparison.

Battery access and internal layout

Orange block: reserved space for an unselected regulator/load-switch circuit. Green block: optional ESP32 space. Neither is an extra printed component.

The raised service pad has been removed. The battery door is a 2.4 mm vertical skin shaped from the original oval underside and set 0.15 mm inward. Its two leading tabs and release catch remain. Physical grip and latch coupons are still essential.
