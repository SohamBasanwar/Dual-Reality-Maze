02 / Adjust. Insert the key. Check the fit.

The fixed corner locates the board. Two jaws set its length and breadth. Pull a locking key sideways, move its jaw, then align and fully reinsert the key. The key carries the sliding load; avoid squeezing the electronics.

The recessed + ↔ − symbols indicate a larger or smaller opening. The nominal setting is the largest front-to-back setting in the handheld bases: the battery bay and adjacent board limit further growth. Main and the control carriers have room on both sides of nominal.

Indexed settings are 3 mm apart. Small nonconductive fit pads may be needed between settings. They must touch safe edges, not solder joints or electronic parts. Open the jaws for installation; only use a position with a complete locking-key passage.

Height uses one matched insert

0.6 mm / 1.2 mm default / 1.8 mm inserts change support height by −0.6 / 0 / +0.6 mm. Use one keyed frame, never a stack. A thicker insert supports a thinner board under the fixed upper lips; a thinner insert accommodates a thicker board. Check that the display, joystick and connectors still align.

The smaller/larger PCB allowances concern board edges. A different shaft offset, LCD glass position or header layout can still require a different carrier.

03 / The controls keep their alignment

RGB retainers keep each LED aligned with its emblem. The battery bay and grip-facing cover retain their dedicated shape. The photoresistor roof is an optical test; a clear-film alternative is supplied if the chosen opaque filament blocks light.

Choose one button contact tip

The snap connection stays the same. Only the lower contact length changes. Compare these with the real installed switch height; the longer option must not preload the switch.

04 / Build the gate, from the inside out

The servo turns its supplied nylon horn. A retained pin travels along the horizontal slot in the gate panel, converting rotation into vertical lift. The frame guides the gate; the removable guard closes the rail ends.
