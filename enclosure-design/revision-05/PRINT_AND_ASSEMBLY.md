# Revision 05 — print and assembly review

The parts are mechanical prototypes. Do not send the complete set for printing until the actual component measurements, test pieces and assigned UIC printer/material are checked. No final printer profile or production G-code is supplied.

## Supports and orientation

Curvature is preserved to match the reference. Supports are allowed where the sliced geometry needs them. They are generated by the slicer, not fused into the CAD.

| Part | Starting orientation | Support and fit review |
|---|---|---|
| Base | Flat underside on the bed | Inspect the grip belly, battery-door ledges, cable saddles and latch openings. Supports must remain reachable from the open shell. |
| Lid | Outside face down, or a modest tilt after preview | Curved grip ends, control wells and the raised decorative rim may need organic/snug supports. Keep supports off the sliding latch faces and small LED keyways where possible. |
| Battery doors | Broad outside face down | Inspect the locating tabs and thin release tongue. Test actual battery/snap space. |
| Emblem masks/diffusers | Broad face down | Preserve light apertures. Use a separate translucent diffuser; opaque bronze plastic will not transmit the status light. |
| RGB keeper | Broad ring face on bed | Tiny quarter-turn tabs need a test with the actual nozzle/material. No supports packed inside the lead passage. |
| Pushbutton cap/cassette | Broad cap face / cassette floor on bed | Verify guide diameter, free return and actual switch travel before operation. |
| Gate panel | Broad face flat | Preserve the yoke slot. Rails and drive pin must move freely after cleanup. |
| Gate frame and shroud | Compare rear face down with upright | Arched regions and internal guide pockets need accessible supports. Do not leave support inside the slide path. |
| USB shutter | Broad plate face down | Keep rails and edges clean; do not scale the part to force a fit. |
| Shutter keeper | Long flat side down | Its top head must survive repeated service. It is held by the closed Main lid. |

Use small test prints before large curved shells. Starting clearances are design assumptions, not universal printer tolerances. PLA, silk PLA, PETG and filled materials do not have interchangeable latch behaviour. Ask the facility which insulating structural polymer is available. Avoid conductive bronze coatings/metal-filled material around an ESP32 antenna.

## Small test pieces

`fit-tests/01` and `02` are the grip battery receiver and door. `03` and `04` test the actual shell latch/receiver. `05`, `06` and `07` test the rear shutter, rails and end stop. Each export is normalized to the build plate. A receiver coupon's cut faces are just sample boundaries; compare the original CAD when assembling.

Reject a fit that needs force, cracks, binds or releases under normal handling. Confirm the battery pull ribbon and snap wire cannot be pinched. A one-colour, low-infill grip trial is also useful before committing to all three housings.

## Screwless assembly

1. Remove supports and deburr edges. Dry-fit the empty shells, battery doors and latch coupons.
2. Fit the common full-size breadboard in its locating tray; retaining fingers must avoid electrical contacts. Retain the UNO with the existing insulated strap anchors and edge cradle. No metal screw bosses are used.
3. Keep the optional ESP32 and jumper reservation clear. Check the actual board's header spacing, connector projection and antenna placement before choosing direct breadboard insertion or a movable carrier with jumpers.
4. Install Explorer's joystick in its underside carrier with screwless straps, then the bare photoresistor in the dedicated accessible opening. Check the joystick's complete swing and press travel. Shield the photoresistor from the RGB emblem's light.
5. Install Architect's potentiometer in its body cradle, the guided button cap and switch cassette. Use the measured shaft and a compatible screwless knob. Fit the SG90 in the removable upper-shoulder cassette, then the frame, panel, supplied horn/linkage and guard. The arched lintel has a hidden travel pocket for the raised panel.
6. Install Main's LCD/backpack and buzzer. Keep LCD header/trimmer access and wire bends clear. Check the real screen against the nominal viewing window. The displayed electronic models are only envelopes.
7. Feed each 5 mm RGB LED through the centre seat from inside. Insert the two-tab keeper through the keyways and turn it 90 degrees to capture the flange. Leads exit through the keeper; insulate them and provide the correct current limiting. Fit diffuser and role mask from the outside. Validate the actual flange, body and leads before printing these small fits.
8. Route the controller tethers through the upper-rear strain-relief saddles, securing the cable jacket rather than bare wires. Keep a service loop for lid removal.
9. In Main, slide the USB cover into the rear guides, insert its end keeper from above, then close the lid to capture the keeper head. Slide the cover open before inserting a USB lead. Confirm that the plug and bend fit the access volume and reach the selected R3/R4 connector; a short internal extension may be needed. The cover is not cable strain relief.
10. Fit the chosen regulated power circuitry, battery snap, insulation and pull ribbon. Keep battery replacement independent of the electronics lid. A printed power cap is not a validated electrical power-switch circuit.
11. Engage the rear lid tabs and side latches. Check all controls with power disconnected first, then test with the proper regulated supplies.

The CAD gate sweep checks the panel against the frame and guard over the proposed motion. It does not certify servo torque, the real horn spline, lead routing or pinch protection. Start powered tests with a small motion range and inspect the mechanism before extending it.
